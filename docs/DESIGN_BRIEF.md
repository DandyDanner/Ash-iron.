# Ash & Iron — Design Brief

## High concept

A stylized first-person frontier crafting-survival adventure. The player begins as a lone frontiersman/trapper beyond established civilization, learns the wilderness, builds a successful homestead, pushes toward dangerous ore-rich mountains, and gradually attracts a handful of capable specialists who see opportunity in what the player has created.

The player is not a mayor. The settlement is a consequence of success, not a management game.

## Core loop

**Prepare → leave home → explore/fight/gather → decide how far to push → haul valuable resources home → process/craft/upgrade → venture farther.**

The best expedition decisions should sound like: "I can probably push farther, but my pack is heavy, my rifle is fouled, the weather is turning, and I have a lot to lose."

## World progression

1. Home valley / meadow — comparatively safe, basic renewable resources
2. Outer forest — first serious predators, ruins, caves, improved materials
3. Deep forest — stronger threats, nighttime danger, rare materials
4. Foothills — climbing, cold, heavier resource hauling, better ore
5. Mountains — major mining territory, rare metals, severe expedition risk

Danger should not be perfectly linear. Safer zones can contain optional high-risk pockets.

## Survival philosophy

- Medium pressure
- Preparation matters
- Mistakes have consequences
- Avoid constant maintenance chores
- Home should feel safe and convenient
- Wilderness logistics are gameplay; base storage logistics are not

## Crafting and resources

Approved material progression: **Stone → Copper → Bronze → Iron → Steel**. Progress should reflect learning new crafts: shaping/lashing, smelting/casting, alloying, forging and refining. The [craftsman progression plan](CRAFTSMAN_PROGRESSION.md) records the stages; the stone/copper slice now includes handcrafting, earned workshop unlocks, copper smelting and a copper axe. Later stages remain planned.

Metals are gathered as ore and must be processed.

Example: **Iron ore → fuel + furnace → iron ingots → forged components/equipment**

Home crafting should automatically use materials in connected storage.

## Weight and hauling

Encumbrance should create expedition decisions without turning into inventory Tetris.

Progression concepts:

- Basic backpack
- Improved backpacks
- Sled (eventually rideable for fun)
- Pack animal
- Cart/wagon only if later testing proves it adds something worthwhile

## Equipment progression

Better tools should improve:

- output
- durability
- efficiency/stamina use
- capability (what materials/tasks are possible)

Late-game gear should be earned through mastery and dangerous access, not low-probability RNG or giant grind walls.

## Maintenance

Keep only maintenance that creates interesting decisions.

Current candidates:

- Firearms: clean → used → fouled → neglected; wet conditions add ignition/reliability risk
- Blades: sharpening and oiling maintain effectiveness and resist neglect/rust

Maintenance should be easy at home and require preparation in the wilderness.

## Skills

Prefer a compact tree with meaningful nodes. Potential branches:

- Combat
- Survival
- Expedition
- Craftsmanship

Avoid long chains of tiny percentage increases. Skills should unlock techniques, efficiencies, and new choices.

## Specialists

Use roughly 3-5 significant NPC specialists in Game 1. They arrive because the homestead creates opportunity. Helping them establish a workspace unlocks meaningful capabilities.

Examples:

- Blacksmith — advanced forging, schematics, equipment modification
- Trapper/hunter — hunting/trapping knowledge, specialized gear
- Prospector/explorer — mining/geology/navigation capabilities
- Trader — access to scarce outside goods

NPCs enhance the player's wilderness loop; they do not automate it away.

## Gear families

Target roughly 2-3 meaningful equipment families rather than a large loot catalog.

Examples:

- Trapper gear — mobility, weather, hunting, carrying
- Forged gear — armor, melee protection, stagger resistance
- Mountain/prospector gear — climbing, cold, expedition logistics

Mixing pieces should remain viable.

## Living forest

Untended ground gradually recovers:

**grass → brush/sticks → saplings → mature trees**

Built, compacted, plowed, cleared, or otherwise maintained ground suppresses regrowth. Small renewable resources may return; major rocks and ore deposits generally remain harvested.

## Narrative shape

The player comes to the frontier seeking solitude/opportunity and gradually falls in love with the wilderness. Evidence of a failed earlier frontier effort creates a central mystery that pulls the player toward the mountains.

Game 1 should resolve its central mystery and culminate at/through the mountains. The ending should feel complete, while revealing that a larger world exists beyond the known frontier.

After credits, the player can continue in the homestead.

## Visual direction

- Clean, stylized, colorful 3D
- Zelda-like readability and atmosphere rather than Valheim's deliberately coarse/pixelated look
- Strong silhouettes and restrained texture noise
- Attractive, lush forest near home
- Darker, colder, more imposing visual language deeper toward the mountains
- Grounded wood, leather, iron, tools, and firearms within a stylized world

## Camera

First-person is the prototype priority. Architect the character so third-person can be added later, but do not double the initial animation/camera workload before the core loop is proven.
